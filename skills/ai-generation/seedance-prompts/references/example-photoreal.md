# Worked examples: photoreal — product and narrative

Two live-action cases. Both lean on the sections that carry feeling and atmosphere, because in photoreal work those are what separate a usable shot from a well-lit nothing.

---

## Why emotion and atmosphere need their own sections

Left unwritten, Seedance defaults to two things that kill photoreal work:

**Performed emotion.** The model gives you wide eyes, raised brows, a held smile — a face announcing a feeling. Real faces do the opposite: they suppress. What reads as genuine on camera is emotion *held* rather than delivered.

**Empty air.** The model gives you clean space between camera and subject. Real spaces have something in the air — haze, dust, steam, smoke, rain, pollen — and that particulate is what builds depth and mood more than any lighting note.

So write both explicitly. The vocabulary that works:

**For acting — describe suppression and timing, not the emotion.**
- `grief held beneath composure` — not "she looks sad"
- `understated, self-conscious, no performing`
- `reactions arrive a half-beat late`
- `smiles start small and grow`
- `she glances away from the lens and back mid-sentence`
- `tears welling without falling` / `exactly one tear falls, hers, at 13.2s`
- `natural blink rate, throat swallows, chest heaving`
- `her gaze never finds the lens` (or: exactly when it does)
- `tension held without release`

Name the feeling once, then spend your words on the physical behavior that carries it. And say what the face must *not* do: `no wide eyes, no exaggerated expressions, no performing.`

**For atmosphere — give the air a material, and the space layers.**
- what's suspended in it: haze, dust motes, steam, drifting spray, fine rain, smoke that always travels away from people
- depth in three layers: foreground / midground / background, each with something in it
- one motivated source, named — a window, a streetlamp, a fire, a phone screen
- how atmosphere changes across the clip (it should; static air reads as a set)
- negative space as a subject: `the space past her shoulder held empty, then filled`
- unretouched surfaces: `visible pores, salt drying to a faint white bloom, sand along the forearms, tired puffy eyes by dawn`

And put ambient life on an **event track** — timed, irregular, never a texture. That's the difference between a room and a photograph of a room.

## Example A — product commercial, real product reference

**References:** `@Image 1` the presenter (identity + wardrobe), `@Image 2` the product, packaging and all, `@Image 3` the location.

**The audit (Step 1):** the product reference is a studio packshot on white. Its backdrop will leak into a location scene unless you say otherwise. And a presenter reference shot in hard studio light will fight a soft-daylight brief — pick the light you want and state that the reference governs identity only.

```
GLOBAL STYLE: Bright commercial-film look, photoreal, crisp daylight
exposure, subject and product razor-sharp with gentle depth falloff on the
background, clean saturated color science. Fine grain, natural micro motion
blur, real optical imperfection — slight halation on highlights, faint edge
softening. NOT a 3D render, NOT glossy synthetic, no beauty retouching, no
skin smoothing. 9:16.

SCENE: A woman finds the product in an ordinary moment, tries it, and quietly
decides she likes it. Nothing dramatic happens.

REFERENCE ROLES:
@Image 1 — presenter identity and wardrobe only. Ignore its lighting and
background; this scene is soft daylight.
@Image 2 — the product exactly: form, materials, label, colors. Ignore its
white studio backdrop. It is the hero object in every shot it appears in and
is never redesigned.
@Image 3 — the location, as-is. All signage unreadable.

CHARACTERS: THE PRESENTER, @Image 1 unchanged — [three hard anchors: hair,
build, wardrobe]. Skin fully unretouched: visible pores, natural sheen, a
faint flush.

ACTING AND EMOTIONAL CONTROL: Understated and slightly self-conscious, like
someone who isn't used to being filmed. Natural blink rate. Reactions arrive
a half-beat late — she registers the taste before her face changes. Smiles
start small and grow. She glances away from the lens and back mid-sentence.
She keeps her voice down. No wide eyes, no exaggerated faces, no performing,
no held camera-ready smile.

ATMOSPHERE: Soft daylight through a window screen-left, one motivated source,
warm cloud fill. Fine dust visible in the light shaft. Three depth layers —
the table edge and a glass in the foreground, her at midground, the room
falling soft behind. The air is not empty.

SHOT / ACTION TIMING: 0.0s–4.0s, SHOT 1, medium, camera 2m, slight handheld
drift. She lifts the @Image 2 product into frame and turns it once, reading
the label. Hard cut. 4.0s–8.5s, SHOT 2, closer. At 5.6s she opens it — real
material sound. At 7.0s the first taste, an unhurried beat before any
reaction. Hard cut. [...]

PHYSICS: Real weight in the hands. Packaging creases and holds its crease.
[Product material behavior — flex, crumb, pour, drape.] Fabric moves with
real weight.

AUDIO: No music. Room ambience continuous — distant traffic through glass,
a fridge hum. The packaging sound at 5.6s. Her voice low, warm, unhurried,
every line a finished sentence, accurate lip-sync. No narration, no subtitles.

LOCKS: Her identity and the @Image 2 product are identical in every frame,
never redesigned, label sharp and correctly spelled whenever visible. The
product only diminishes, never regrows. Exactly one person exists. Five
fingers per hand. No on-screen text, no end packshot — the clip ends on her.
```

**What carries the ad:** the half-beat delay and the unhurried taste. A reaction that arrives instantly reads as an actor; a reaction that arrives late reads as a person.

## Example B — cinematic narrative, emotion held

Two people, one exchange, atmosphere doing the emotional work.

```
GLOBAL STYLE: Photoreal large-format cinema, anamorphic character, oval
bokeh, slight corner softening. Cold naturalistic light with a single warm
accent. Fine 35mm grain, gate weave, halation only around the source, deep
blacks with a milky filmic floor. Skin unretouched and hyper-detailed. NOT a
clean CGI render, no glossy sheen, no beauty retouch. 2.39:1.

SCENE: Two people who are about to be separated sit together in the last of
the light and say almost nothing.

EMOTIONAL CONTROL: Grief held beneath composure. Neither breaks. Tension is
held for the full runtime and never released. Both carry tears standing in
the eyes throughout — exactly one tear falls, hers, at 13.2s. Micro-acting
only: natural blinks, one throat swallow each, a breath caught and let go.
No sobbing, no embrace, no raised voices. The only touch in the film is
their shoulders settling together at 14.2s.

ATMOSPHERE AND EVENT TRACK: The air carries cold spray and drifting mist —
the environment is an event track, not a texture, with irregular intervals so
no two beats read alike. 1.2s a wave strikes the forward rocks, spray to 12%
of frame height drifting inland. 2.4s a gust lifts hair and the free edges of
both coats, then settles. 5.0s a smaller impact, its hiss fading out just
before she speaks — a relative lull holds under her line. 7.6s the strongest
gust, both bodies brace a degree into it. 13.8s the largest impact, a low
thud felt through the ground, spray hanging a beat in the backlight.

LIGHTING: One real source, low and warm, unmoving for the full runtime,
against a cool pale skylight fill from above. Shots from behind read them as
two dark shapes rimmed in warm light; frontal shots carry a full low key on
the faces, wet eyes holding one small intense catchlight each.

STILLNESS LOCK: Both remain seated for the entire clip. Nobody stands, nobody
embraces, nobody's hands leave their pockets.

AUDIO: Continuous ambience, impacts exactly at the event-track times, steady
wind buffet with the two gusts, one distant bird call at 8.6s. The two
scripted lines are the only words spoken. No music, no narration, no subtitles.
```

**What carries the scene:** the STILLNESS LOCK and the lull at 5.0s. Forbidding the embrace is what makes the restraint read as restraint, and dropping the ambient level under her line is a sound decision doing emotional work — cheaper and more reliable than asking the model to act harder.

---

## The transferable rule

In photoreal work, feeling comes from what you forbid and atmosphere comes from what you suspend in the air. Both are prompt sections, not happy accidents — and both are the first thing to add when a render comes back technically correct and emotionally flat.
